DETOUR 01 · LIFE CHOICES ACADEMY

01

Too much road.
Too little time.

During my early months studying ICT at Life Choices Academy, I often felt burned out. The workload was intense, time was limited, and just as I started adapting to one concept or learning topic, another one would arrive.

I had to learn how to manage my time and activities instead of letting the pressure overwhelm me. I gradually became better at prioritising tasks, organising my workload and making progress within deadlines.

Today, I can complete tasks on time and juggle different responsibilities without constantly feeling overwhelmed or believing that I am falling behind.

LESSON LEARNED Manage the journey, not just the deadline.

DETOUR 02 · DIFFERENT DRIVERS, DIFFERENT ROUTES

02

I stopped racing
other drivers.

There were times when I felt pressured by teammates who seemed to understand concepts faster or move ahead of me. Comparing my progress with theirs made me feel as though I was being left behind.

Eventually, I realised that each person was on their own journey. We could be travelling towards similar destinations without being at the same point on the road. My real responsibility was not to compete with my teammates, but to keep learning and make sure I continued moving towards my own destination.

That change in perspective helped me approach learning with more patience and focus. I could appreciate other people's progress without treating it as evidence of my own failure.

LESSON LEARNED My progress is measured by my growth, not someone else's speed.

DETOUR 03 · WHEN CONFIDENCE BREAKS DOWN

03

The imposter syndrome tunnel.

When I became stuck in a coding problem and could not see how to move forward, I sometimes began questioning whether technology was really for me. Those thoughts created a kind of tunnel vision: I became so focused on what I could not solve that I struggled to see the resources and people around me.

I had to learn that being stuck does not mean that I do not belong in technology. Sometimes I need to step back, rethink the problem, research the concept or explain what I do not understand to someone else.

Most importantly, I learned to ask for help. My colleagues were fellow drivers on the journey, people who could help me change a tyre when I could not get the car moving again. I did not have to solve every problem in isolation.

LESSON LEARNED Being stuck is a moment in the journey, not my identity.

THE PEOPLE TRAVELLING ALONGSIDE ME

A good driver knows when to pull over and ask.

Learning to ask for help changed more than how I approached code. It taught me to value other people's perspectives, communicate when I need support and recognise that collaboration makes difficult journeys easier to navigate.

I bring that lesson into teamwork: I listen, ask questions, share what I know and understand that a team's progress is not a race between individuals. We can help one another reach the destination.

NO DRIVER LEFT BEHIND

DETOUR 04 · A PRACTICAL TECHNICAL LESSON

04

Wrong turn on GitHub.

During a team project, I accidentally deleted team work while editing because I had not realised I was on the main branch instead of my own branch.

I reached out to my team leader, and we worked through the issue. The experience taught me to pay closer attention to my current branch, understand the state of my changes and be careful when working in a shared codebase.

It also reinforced a lesson that ran through my other challenges: when something goes wrong, acknowledge it, communicate and work towards a solution rather than allowing fear to stop you.

LESSON LEARNED Check your branch before you change the road.
