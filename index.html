Drawn by hand. Built with code.

Khayelitsha, Cape Town HTML & CSS JavaScript Node & Express MySQL

I learned to see structure by drawing it. A face is a set of planes, a shadow is a gradient you have to earn, and nothing looks right until you erase and go back over it. That instinct turned out to be the most useful thing I had when I started writing software.

Everything here was built the slow way. Broken layouts, rewrites, a group project where my module only worked once the database agreed with it. The work is unfinished on purpose, because it keeps moving.

I dream my painting and I paint my dream.

Vincent van Gogh

Why this exists

A sketchbook, not a showroom

Most portfolios show finished work. This one shows the work in progress, because the part I am actually good at is the iteration.

Drawing taught me to commit to a line before I know whether it is right. Coding asked me to commit to a structure before I know whether it holds weight. Both turn out to be the same discipline: make a decision, look at the result honestly, and correct it without defending it.

The projects in the Workshop are the record. Some are front-end studies I built to understand layout. One is a real full-stack HR system where I wrote part of the backend and had to make the front end actually talk to it. Nothing is dressed up beyond what the code shows.

I am still in training, and I would rather be honest about that than inflate it. What I can tell you is that I understand how the pieces fit together, and I care about getting them right.
